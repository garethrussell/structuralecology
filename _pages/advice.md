## Advice for High School and Undergraduate Students

If you have been directed to this page it is likely because you sent me an e-mail expressing interest in doing research in my lab, but also sent an almost identical request to one or more of my colleagues in the same Department, perhaps changing a few words to match their lab’s research area.

In my Department we have group chats where we check these things, but even without that it is almost always obvious from the e-mail itself that a 'form letter' has been used.

Let me commend you on your initiative, but also let you know that this is not the way to go about it. Most obviously, it makes your expression of interest in any specific research area appear insincere. All of us get many requests for research opportunities — far more than we can take on — and so we must be very selective. While the use of a form letter may let you contact many more researchers, it makes each of us *less* likely to respond favorably. I myself won't consider a request further.

So what *should* you do to convince a busy faculty member to consider your application? You need to do the work to demonstrate your interest in that lab's research area. That means taking the time to go over the lab website to understand what that group does, and reading some recent publications (at least the abstract, introduction and discussion). If you are genuinely still interested, then you can write and say so, using your own words and showing that you have put in the effort. (Do not use AI to craft your letter: we can sense that too and it will work against you in most cases.) If, after you dig into it, the research does not seem interesting to you, do not write that letter! Move on and look for something that does excite you. Research should be driven by genuine curiosity, otherwise it is no fun.

Doing this provides no guarantee that any given researcher will take you on. They just might not have the capacity. But it will increase your chances over the 'blast email' approach. So with this advice in hand, get back out there, read widely, and think hard and honestly about what you like. Good luck!
